# Generative models and doctoral writing practice

## Rationale and positioning with regard to the state-of-the-art

Studies of generative models in doctoral writing rely on surveys of declared use, not on observed drafts or supervision talk (VUB, Central Doctoral Regulations, 2025; National survey of doctoral candidates, 2024; Review of university AI policies in Flanders, 2026).

## Scientific research goals

How do doctoral candidates change their writing practices once generative language models enter supervision? Scope: Four Flemish universities; doctoral candidates in the humanities and social sciences in their first two years; 2026 to 2029. What would count as an answer: The question is answered when a typology of writing practices, built from observed supervision meetings and successive drafts, accounts for the variation found across the four sites.

Hypothesis: Candidates shift from sentence-level revision to prompt-and-verify cycles, and the shift is uneven across disciplines.

## Research methodology and work plan

Multi-site ethnography of supervision, combined with a longitudinal corpus of drafts and their revision histories. Sources: Observed supervision meetings, four universities, 2026-2029 (data); Draft corpus with version histories, contributed by participating candidates (corpus); University policy documents on AI use in doctoral education, 2023-2026 (corpus).

Follow 24 candidates, six per university, for two years; observe four supervision meetings per candidate per year; collect every draft of one chapter with its version history; code revision operations and prompt-and-verify sequences; build the typology by thematic analysis; member-check the typology with participants in the third year.

Validity conditions: Candidates are followed from before their first sustained use of a generative model; Drafts come from a single chapter per candidate, so revisions are comparable; Field notes and transcripts are coded by two researchers independently. Limits: The sample covers humanities and social sciences only, where drafting is solitary and text-centred; Candidates who refuse to share drafts are absent from the corpus; Observed meetings may differ from unobserved ones.

The procedure delivers: A coding scheme for revision operations and prompt-and-verify sequences, with a codebook and worked examples.
