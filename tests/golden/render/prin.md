# Digital-health platforms and equity in avoidable admissions

## a. Objectives, state of the art, methodology and risks

Evaluations of Italian digital-health platforms stop at single-region case studies and do not test whether the recorded reduction reaches low-income patients (Ministry of Health, hospital discharge records, 2024 release; MUR, PRIN 2026 evaluation guidelines, D.D. 2298 All. 3; OECD, Health at a Glance: Europe, 2024).

To what extent do regional digital-health platforms reduce avoidable hospital admissions for patients with chronic conditions, and how is that reduction distributed across income groups? Criteria: the reduction in avoidable admissions per 1,000 chronic patients; the ratio of that reduction in the lowest income quintile to the highest. Operationalisation: Avoidable admissions are the ambulatory-care-sensitive conditions set, counted per 1,000 registered chronic patients per year; equity is the ratio of the platform effect in the lowest income quintile to the effect in the highest, with the quintile assigned from the patient's exemption category. Scope: Six Italian regions that activated a platform between 2019 and 2023; patients aged 18 and over with at least one chronic condition; 2016 to 2026. What would count as an answer: The question is answered when a region-level effect on avoidable admissions is estimated against non-adopting regions and the equity ratio is reported with its confidence interval.

Staggered difference-in-differences on administrative hospital records, with event-study checks and a synthetic control for the earliest adopter. Sources: Hospital discharge records, 2016-2026, Ministry of Health (data); Chronic-disease exemption registry, 2016-2026, regional health agencies (data); Platform activation dates and covered services, 2019-2023, regional health agencies (data).

Define the six treated regions and the eligible control regions; aggregate discharges to region-year cells by condition set and income quintile; estimate the effect of activation with region and year fixed effects; inspect event-study leads for pre-trends; repeat the estimate for each quintile and report the ratio; run a synthetic control for the 2019 adopter as a robustness check.

Validity conditions: Regions are compared before and after activation, with non-adopting regions as controls; Discharge coding stays stable across the period, checked on a fixed set of diagnoses; Income quintile is observed for every patient through the exemption category. Limits: The design estimates the effect of activation, not of platform use intensity; Regions that activated before 2019 fall outside the comparison; Avoidable admissions measure access, not the clinical quality of ambulatory care; The equity ratio is sensitive to how patients without an exemption are assigned.

## b. Pathways to results and expected impacts

Expected finding: Activation reduces avoidable admissions by between 3 and 6 per cent, and the reduction is smaller in the lowest income quintile than in the highest.

The procedure delivers: An open dataset of region-level avoidable-admission rates by condition set and income quintile, 2016-2026, with the code that builds it.
