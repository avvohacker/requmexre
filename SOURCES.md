## Impact

Impact is not one of the three slots. The model has no impact field: `results` and
`standpoint` can inform an impact section, but neither is an impact narrative. The
working definition is:

> Impact is a change, effect or benefit — positive or negative, intended or
> unintended — that research contributes to produce beyond its direct outputs,
> in a specified domain (scientific, economic, social, cultural, environmental,
> policy), through a pathway of use, adoption or valorisation. It is expected
> ex ante and realised ex post; it is a contribution, not necessarily a sole
> attribution.

The definition is deliberately minimal because the sources differ: REF defines
impact beyond academia; UKRI/ESRC includes academic impact; Horizon separates
results, outcomes and impacts; PRIN separates the excellence criterion from the
impact criterion; SNSF asks for scientific relevance and expected impacts for
the discipline and academic learning (research and education/teaching).

### Results, outcomes and impacts: a common reference

The OECD DAC glossary supplies the general vocabulary that the six forms use
unevenly: *results* is the umbrella term for outputs, outcomes or impacts;
*outcomes* are the short- or medium-term effects of outputs; *impacts* are the
higher-level effects of outcomes. The 2023 second edition changed the 2002
wording: *outcome* is no longer “likely or achieved”, and *impacts* are defined
as higher-level effects of outcomes, distinct from the impact criterion. It is a
development-evaluation vocabulary, not a funder template; the matrix uses it
only to keep the terms apart.

|---|---|---|
| OECD DAC, *Glossary of Key Terms in Evaluation and Results-Based Management for Sustainable Development* (2nd ed., 2023) | Results = outputs, outcomes or impacts (intended or unintended, positive or negative); outcomes = short-term and medium-term effects of outputs; impacts = higher-level effects of outcomes, including ultimate or longer-term changes; the impact criterion is separate. | [PDF](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/06/glossary-of-key-terms-in-evaluation-and-results-based-management-for-sustainable-development-second-edition_2767e14e/632da462-en-fr-es.pdf) |

## Results, outcomes and impacts in the six funders

“Outcome” is the analytical middle term: a change in knowledge, behaviour,
capacity or uptake. Horizon uses it as a defined middle term; FWO calls its
expected results “expected outcomes”; PRIN, SNSF, DFG and VUB do not use
“outcome” as a separate term. “Not a separate term” means the form does not
distinguish it, not that no such change is expected. A cell saying “no separate
section” means the form has no dedicated section.

## Conceptual sources

The three headings are a writing heuristic, not a standard. These guides explain
the terms in general research-design and proposal-writing practice; they do not
define requmexre, and none is normative for the schema. Links checked 22 November
2026.

### What the three terms mean

- **Research question.** The uncertainty the study proposes to reduce, arising
  from a gap or problem. It is not a topic, a method or a hypothesis: a hypothesis
  is one possible answer, and an objective is an operational statement of what the
  study will do. A good question is answerable and bounded; the common criteria are
  FINER (feasible, interesting, novel, ethical, relevant) and, in clinical research,
  PICOT. The model's `questions.primary` carries the text, form, answer type, scope
  and completion criterion; `questions.secondary` carries the rest.
- **Methodology.** The justified design for producing an answer, not a list of
  techniques. Creswell separates the research approach (methodology), the research
  design within it, and the methods of data collection, analysis and interpretation;
  the proposal guides add sources, procedure, validity conditions, limits and risks.
  The model's `method` holds the approach, sources and procedure; the optional
  conditions, limits and risks make the design defensible.
- **Expected results.** The results the study anticipates and can state, not the
  longer-term impact it hopes to have. A claim (hypothesis or expected finding) is
  testable and carries a falsifier and the evidence needed; an outcome is a quantity
  with a measure; an output is a produced artefact. Logic-model guidance groups
  outputs, outcomes and impact as “intended results” but distinguishes direct
  products, changes and long-term impact; the model keeps outputs and outcomes,
  adds testable claims, and leaves impact outside. “Expected” does not mean guaranteed, and forms call these
  anticipated findings, intended outcomes or expected contribution. The model's
  `results` holds claims, outcomes and outputs; impact stays outside.

The model's five content blocks formalise this division: question and warrant,
method, results, standpoint. The sources do not agree on one vocabulary, which is
why the schema has `form`, result kinds and section mappings rather than a fixed
prose template.

The vocabulary varies: Salford and Johannesburg say **Expected results**, Guelph
says **Anticipated findings**, Portsmouth says **Intended outcomes**, and the
logic-model literature separates **outputs**, **outcomes** and **impact**. That
variation is why the model uses one field, `results`, with result kinds, and why a
form's heading is mapped rather than renamed.

## Note on method

No document in the funder tables states the three slots as a standard. They are
enacted in forms and guidelines, not argued in print. The conceptual guides above
explain the three terms separately; they do not make the triad a standard. The
funder tables record what each document requires, not a theory that the documents
share.
