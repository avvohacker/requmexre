## Impact

Impact is not one of the three slots. The model has no impact field: `results` and
`standpoint` can inform an impact section, but neither is an impact narrative. The
working definition is:

> Impact is a change, effect or benefit — positive or negative, intended or
> unintended — that research contributes to produce beyond its direct outputs,
> in a specified domain (scientific, economic, social, cultural, environmental,
> policy), through a pathway of use, adoption or valorisation. It is expected
> ex ante and realised ex post; it is a contribution, not necessarily a sole
> attribution.

The definition is deliberately minimal because the sources differ: REF defines
impact beyond academia; UKRI/ESRC includes academic impact; Horizon separates
results, outcomes and impacts; PRIN separates the excellence criterion from the
impact criterion; SNSF asks for scientific relevance and expected impacts for
the discipline and academic learning (research and education/teaching).

### Results, outcomes and impacts: a common reference

The OECD DAC glossary supplies the general vocabulary that the six forms use
unevenly: *results* is the umbrella term for outputs, outcomes or impacts;
*outcomes* are the short- or medium-term effects of outputs; *impacts* are the
higher-level effects of outcomes. The 2023 second edition changed the 2002
wording: *outcome* is no longer “likely or achieved”, and *impacts* are defined
as higher-level effects of outcomes, distinct from the impact criterion. It is a
development-evaluation vocabulary, not a funder template; the matrix uses it
only to keep the terms apart.

| Source | What it establishes | Link |
|---|---|---|
| OECD DAC, *Glossary of Key Terms in Evaluation and Results-Based Management for Sustainable Development* (2nd ed., 2023) | Results = outputs, outcomes or impacts (intended or unintended, positive or negative); outcomes = short-term and medium-term effects of outputs; impacts = higher-level effects of outcomes, including ultimate or longer-term changes; the impact criterion is separate. | [PDF](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/06/glossary-of-key-terms-in-evaluation-and-results-based-management-for-sustainable-development-second-edition_2767e14e/632da462-en-fr-es.pdf) |

## Results, outcomes and impacts in the six funders

“Outcome” is the analytical middle term: a change in knowledge, behaviour,
capacity or uptake. Horizon uses it as a defined middle term; FWO calls its
expected results “expected outcomes”; PRIN, SNSF, DFG and VUB do not use
“outcome” as a separate term. “Not a separate term” means the form does not
distinguish it, not that no such change is expected. A cell saying “no separate
section” means the form has no dedicated section.

The matrix is a reading of the forms, not a claim that all six use one
vocabulary. It supports the mapping: results sections map `results`; outcome is a
`results` kind where the model has one; impact stays outside the model.
`standpoint` is not impact: it holds the normative premises and criteria.

## Note on method

No document in this corpus states the three slots as a standard. They are enacted in forms and guidelines, not argued in print. The tables above record what each document requires, not a theory that the documents share.
